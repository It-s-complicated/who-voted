# Election rules and official evidence

Checked on 2026-09-14 for the 17 elections currently included in the app. These references support the minimum voting age and the vote category used by the diagram. They are election-specific publications or official pages explicitly describing the named election.

The executable settings and source references live in [scripts/election-rules.mjs](scripts/election-rules.mjs). An unconfigured election is rejected instead of inheriting rules from its state. Berlin, Bremen and Hamburg retain their dedicated election-specific parsers; the data checks compare their output with these settings too.

“Votes per voter” below means the maximum number in the **displayed category**: one for a second/list vote, two for Bavaria's combined first and second votes, five for Hamburg's state-list ballot or Bremen's parliament ballot. It is not the total number of choices across all ballots.

The Thüringen 2024 label is corrected to **Landesstimmen**, matching its official ballot terminology. Sachsen's publication explicitly calls its list vote **Listenstimme (Zweitstimme)**, so the existing Zweitstimmen label remains supported.

Result-column indices describe document layouts, not election law. Their evidence is the retained results file and checksum in `data/raw/<state>/<year>/sources.json`; `resultColumn` is zero-based. PDF/spreadsheet layouts remain in the dedicated parser branches and still need checking before another election is added.

For a new election, verify its age and vote system against an official source for that election, add its exact route and references, and inspect its retained result-table headers before choosing a column. A current state's FAQ alone does not establish historical rules. Rule references here are links, not checksum-pinned local snapshots; the existing raw-file manifests continue to cover results and population inputs.
